## Introduction

## 1 Approval and valid procedure

This procedure applies to towing with the DaWinchi ground winch operated by Voss HPK.
The procedure must be approved by NLF/HPS before towing activity begins.
All pilots, operators and assistants taking part in the activity must know the parts of the procedure that apply to their roles.
The procedure must have a revision number and date.
When equipment, the winch, local conditions or working methods change, it must be assessed whether the procedure needs to be updated and approved again.

### 1.1 Towing site and area

Sites used regularly for towing must be approved for this by NLF/HPS.
At Voss, we have these sites:

- Lotatreet
- Istad
- Dyrvedalen

Before the activity, the person responsible for towing must check that:

- landowner permission has been clarified
- the NOTAM is valid for the site, time and planned altitude
- launch and landing areas are suitable and clear of obstacles
- the towing direction and line route provide sufficient clearance from roads, buildings, power lines, people and other obstacles
- alternative landing options are available in the event of a line break or aborted winching

### 1.2 Flying and movement in the towing area

During active winching, the tow path and the area around the line must be kept clear of unauthorized people, vehicles and other activity.
Other pilots must keep well clear of the active tow line and must not cross the tow path while towing is in progress.
Another tow must not begin until the airspace, launch area and line area are clear.
If needed, the area must be secured with assistants, signs or other suitable marking.
The person responsible for the activity must ensure that a valid NOTAM is in place for the towing site and the period during which the activity takes place.

## 1 Competence and roles during towing

For towing, the pilot must hold a qualification for paragliding towing (OTP), or be training to obtain it.
Ordinary PP2–PP5 qualification is not in itself sufficient to be towed.
Beginners can nevertheless be trained using towing from the first day as part of organized training.

### 2.1 Pilot - OTP

OTP is additional training for paragliding towing.
The pilot must be able to take care of their own safety and that of others during towing, and must master:

- the correct release procedure
- the correct position in relation to the towing device
- speed and direction corrections
- handling rapid changes in climb rate and position due to wind and thermals

The pilot must also have knowledge of the forces acting during the different phases of towing, the tow line, weak link/break link, release system, the function and limitations of the towing device, as well as procedures, signals and emergency procedures.
To obtain OTP, the pilot must have completed at least 10 approved tows.

The OTP qualification is renewed together with the ordinary qualification.
To maintain towing competence, the minimum requirement is 5 tows during the previous 60 months.

### 2.2 Operator of the towing device

The operator of the winch/towing device must hold a qualification for the specific device.
The qualification is therefore tied to the towing device itself and must not be confused with the pilot's OTP qualification.
Operator training must:

- be given by a person who is themselves trained on the device
- be documented in writing
- include at least 10 successful tows under supervision and instruction before a qualification can be issued.

An operator with at least 50 successful tows using another towing device may operate a new towing device.

### 2.3 Towing instructor - PIO

OTP training and proficiency checks must be conducted by a person with the required instructor qualification.
PIO - paragliding towing instructor - is a separate additional qualification for instructors.
To become a PIO, a candidate must:

1. hold a paragliding instructor qualification (PI)
2. hold OTP
3. have completed at least 20 tows
4. have instructed during an OTP proficiency check under the supervision of a PIO and been approved.

### 2.4 Person responsible for the towing activity

For towing, a named person with a towing qualification must be responsible for the operation.
The operator of the towing device must also ensure that ground assistants have the knowledge needed to perform their tasks safely.
This means that a launch assistant or other helper does not necessarily need a separate “launch-assistant certificate”, but that person must be instructed and have the necessary knowledge of the task they perform.

### 2.5 Ground crew and launch

There must be enough ground crew to keep unauthorized people at a safe distance from the activity.
All pilots who are to be towed must have familiarized themselves with the approved towing procedure before flying.

## 3 Local activity - DaWinchi ground winch

Everyone who uses this type of winch must receive sufficient training from the club's appointed towing leads.
At present, Lars Haukom, Morten Knapstad and Kjell Svanes have the most experience with the club's winch and local towing sites, and hold operator and towing instructor qualifications.
One of them must therefore be responsible for the activity.
If more people gain the same type of qualification, the responsible group may be expanded gradually.

### 3.1 Training new operators, OTP pilots and instructors

Training takes place only when a local towing instructor or local winch operator is present.
OTP training must be conducted by a towing instructor and formal course registration must be in order.
A towing instructor may train a new winch operator if an experienced OTP pilot is flying; the recommended OTP pilot has completed at least 50 tows (local guideline).
A newly qualified OTP pilot must not fly when the operator is also in training.
This gives both parties the best learning outcome.
General recommendation: join first as ground crew and gradually become familiar with components, checklists and routines to gain an understanding of the activity.
When you have a good overview and the correct roles in place, you can gradually expand your area of responsibility.
It helps understanding if a person who is to become a winch operator already has experience with towing as an OTP pilot.
Help check and fit the equipment for the pilot who is flying.
After a while, it is natural to carry out the first tow.
If shorter flights are made to train the operator, an experienced OTP pilot with good mental capacity and wing control must fly.

### 3.2 Activity planning

- Clarify who is taking part and has overall responsibility for the activity?
  Correct qualifications and responsible roles.
- Always check the forecast - days with crosswind or wind speeds above 5 m/s should be avoided.
- Check with all landowners at the planned site.
  This is done for each activity.
- Request a NOTAM 24 hours before use, no later than 16:00 the day before.
  If necessary, call the skydiving club and gliding club on the day you are operating.
  They can see us on the SafeSky app.
  Enter the call sign “Paraglider winch to .... m a.s.l.”.
- Put the winch on charge the evening before; it takes 4–5 hours.
  It must not be charged just before use!
- Pack the car with the winch equipment and first-aid equipment.

### 3.3 Equipment requirements and daily check

Before the first tow of the day, the operator and pilot are responsible for checking the equipment and safety system.
Among other things, check that:

- the pilot's release system is correctly fitted and works
- the release is easily accessible during every phase of the tow
- the release has been inspected and function-tested
- the winch has a working emergency release or line cutter
- the line, line guide, attachment and drum have no visible damage
- any weak link/break link is suitable for the system in question
- radio communication and the remote control work where used

The safety system requires an effective and easily accessible release that the pilot can activate throughout the tow, and that the towing device has an emergency release or line cutter.
Releases must be inspected and tested daily

### 3.4 Accident and emergency response

First-aid equipment must be available at the launch area before activity begins.

In the event of an accident:

1. Stop the towing activity.
2. Secure the area and remove any danger from the line, winch or traffic.
3. Give the necessary first aid.
4. Call 113 in the event of serious injury or danger to life.
5. State the location, type of incident and that the incident concerns paragliding/winching.
6. Notify the Technical Director in Voss HPK.
7. Follow the club's action and notification plan.
8. The incident must be reported under the current NLF/HPS procedures.

### 3.5 Preparing the winch

Images taken from the user manual.
They show the winch attached to the tow hitch, control unit and battery, and the antenna.

![DaWinchi mounted on a car and tow hitch](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vosshpk/wiki/main/assets/landvinsj-transport.jpg "DaWinchi mounted on a car")

- All users of this unit need the correct qualification for towing.
  This applies to the winch operator and pilot/towing student, as well as the towing instructor.
- Have great respect for the line when using DaWinchi.
  Do not touch any moving parts.
- Make a new assessment of the forecast and actual weather conditions.
  Crosswind or wind speeds above 5m/s should be avoided.
- Apply the car's parking brake before towing.
- Always keep a professional line cutter within reach during towing - both by the winch and in the cockpit.
- Use DaWinchi at your own risk and do not let anyone operate it unless they are a certified and experienced towing operator.
- First-aid equipment must be where the tow starts at “start”.
- Do not use a pickup bed or anything that sticks out, as the winch rises during the tow.

### 3.6 Technical check

Make sure that the control box is set to INT and the INT slider is at 0 before and after use, so that it does not start spinning by itself during setup!

![DaWinchi control box and components](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vosshpk/wiki/main/assets/landvinsj-kontrollboks.jpg "Control box and components")

- INT must be the setting at startup and shutdown.
  The INT slider must be to the left.
- The line must be taped / or secured in other ways when testing.
- Start slowly and stop slowly while winding in during use.
  At the end of the day, the line should be winched in firmly (at full speed).
  When winding in the line, one must stop in good time.
  Those who are to become winch operators should have tried this.
- Wear on the loop.
  When it becomes frayed, it should be repaired.
  A knife and splicing materials should be in the box.
- There must be a knife for cutting the line by the motor.
- Always tension the line before it is connected to the pilot.
- Maintain the battery when it is not going to be used.

### 3.7 Setup

1. Place the control box on the right and the battery on the left.
2. Connect the antenna to the five-pin connector on top of the control box.
3. Put the receiver on the antenna on the telescopic pole and extend it.
   Secure the pole in the ground.
4. Insert the red key and make sure it points to “OFF”.
   Set the on/off button to “0”.
5. Open the box.
   Set the small switch to the left (INT).
   The spool is now driven locally.
6. Connect the battery to the control box.
7. Check the control voltage on the box.
   It should build up voltage, and when it slows around 60– 70 V, the main power can be switched on with the key.
   If this is done too early, it may damage the controller.
   The displayed voltage should now be around 80–100 V depending on battery type and charge.
8. Attach the winch mount to the tow hitch ball.
   Tighten the wing nuts and check that it can swivel.
   Use grease/oil to improve the movement.
9. Slide the DaWinchi onto the mount, insert the large and small bolts and secure them.
10. Make sure the sleeve is centered and does not make a noise when you rotate the spool by hand.
11. Attach the line guide and tighten the screws and wing nut.
    The line build-up should be in the middle of the spool.
    Adjust it after the first few tows if necessary.
    The line must be mounted in the middle.
    Adjust the line guide by loosening the bolt and moving it so that the line is centered.
12. Insert the large motor plug on the side of the controller and the six-pin control plug.
13. Turn on the controller by pressing the black button to “1”.
    DaWinchi is now ready to spin!
14. Use the throttle slider marked “INT” to rotate the spool.
    Tape the line to the side of the spool and test at full speed.
16. By switching to RC, you can alternate between the green and orange remote controls.
17. Run the line from the spool through the pulley.
    Attach the brake parachute with the carabiner.
    Use the retrieval vehicle to pull the line out (slowly) for takeoff.
    DaWinchi is now ready for towing!

## 4 Operator instructions - winching with an operator on the ground

- Check the radio signal at launch by holding the line (do not wrap the line around your hand!) and applying a little throttle.
- Do not jerk the line, as this may cause tangles.
- Attach the line to the pilot and carry out an equipment check with a double check.
- Build up a light pull on the line and let the pilot's corresponding forward movement pull the wing up.
- When the wing is stable and the pilot is in control, increase the throttle according to wind strength.
- Be careful with the throttle until the pilot has reached a safe height, at least 30–50 metres above the ground.
  Follow the angle of attack throughout the tow, and control the pitch/movement of the wing by adjusting the pull with the throttle.
- When the pilot has reached the desired height, release the throttle.
  The controller will automatically reduce the line pull gradually so that the pilot can release.
- Be aware that there is a 3-second delay from when the throttle is released.

Maintain a steady speed as the line is wound in.
Avoid stopping and starting while winding in, as this can cause tangles.
The line must not be wound in without resistance/tension.

### 4.1 Emergency procedures - winch operator

In an abnormal situation, the winch operator must prioritize reducing or removing the pulling force.

#### Aborted launch

- Release the throttle/pull immediately.
- Check that the line goes slack.
- Do not begin another tow until the cause has been clarified.

#### Pilot veers off course

- Reduce the pull so that the pilot has a better opportunity to correct.
- If the deviation increases or there is a risk of lockout, the pull must be terminated.

#### Risk of lockout

- Remove the pulling force immediately.
- Use the emergency release or line cutter if the line is not released.

#### Loss of radio or remote-control signal

- Stop or reduce the pull if the next part of the tow cannot be carried out in a controlled manner.
- During self-winching, the pilot must follow their own emergency procedure for signal loss.

#### Line or drum locks

- Stop the winch.
- If the pilot is still connected and the line cannot be slackened, use the emergency release or line cutter.

#### Technical fault

- Stop the tow.
- Do not use the winch again until the fault has been identified and corrected.

## 5 Self-winching

During self-winching, the pilot has full control of the winching and is therefore responsible for carrying it out safely.
Self-winching must only be carried out if the pilot has the necessary qualifications both as a pilot being towed and to operate the specific towing device.
During self-winching, the pilot is both the pilot and the person controlling the towing device.
The pilot must therefore be particularly familiar with:

- the remote control and emergency shutdown
- loss of radio signal
- delay in regulating the pulling force
- checking the line and brake parachute
- aborted launch
- alternative landing
- emergency release and line cutting

The safety system requires both a qualification for a participant in towing and a qualification for the operator of the relevant towing device.
Self-winching at Voss HPK is carried out only after approval and training from one of the club's local responsible towing instructors.

Some important points:

- Do not switch on the remote control before the wing is fully inflated.
  Then build up a light line pull, take off and select the desired climb rate with the throttle.
- Attach the strap on the remote control to the hand you prefer.
  Put your wrist through the brake loop and point the remote control toward the antenna.
  Steer the wing by moving your arm up and down.
- Be careful with the throttle in strong wind.
- If you abort the launch: land and walk back to the launch with the line to avoid tangles.
- Make sure the remote control always points toward the antenna.
- If the connection is broken, the remote control will continue with the current throttle setting when the connection returns.
  Therefore, keep the throttle low until the connection is restored.
- If the remote control is switched off, the signal stops and the winch stops pulling.
- Release the throttle at the end of the tow and wait until the controller has reduced the line pull enough before releasing.
- Keep an eye on the brake parachute while the line is being wound in.
- Turn off the remote control and continue flying.

## 6 Pilot instructions for winching - PG

Winching is an effective way to get many flights.
Safe and effective winching requires good cooperation between the pilot, launch assistant and winch operator.
The pilot is always responsible for their own flying and equipment.

- The paraglider must be approved for towing by the manufacturer and free of damage.
- The harness must be free of damage and have at least one reserve parachute repacked during the last 12 months.
- An approved release must be used, and it must be free of damage.

### 6.1 Before winching

Report to the launch assistant when you arrive in the area and put your name on the queue list.

- Prepare the wing and equipment while you wait.
- Help with the line, equipment and other preparations.
- Be ready when it is your turn.

Winching is teamwork - everyone must contribute.

### 6.2 Check before start

Before you connect to the winch line, check:

- Do the five-point check, and check that no lines are caught on the harness/shoes or similar.
- The wing and harness are correctly connected
- All buckles are closed and locked
- The helmet strap is fastened
- The release system is correctly installed and works
- The weak link/break link is correctly rated

For PG, the weak link must be rated at 75% of the hooked-in weight (pilot + equipment).
The launch assistant can help with the check, but the pilot is responsible for ensuring that the equipment is correct and ready before takeoff.

### 6.3 Start

When you, the launch assistant and winch operator are ready, the command “Køyr vinsj” (“Run the winch”) is given.
When the line tightens:

- Follow the pull from the line calmly forward.
- Do not resist the pull, but avoid running so that the line becomes slack.
- Prioritize control of the wing and direction.
- Give the wing full flying speed, and correct on only one side at a time if necessary.
- Move your gaze forward as soon as the wing has come nicely into balance above your head.
- Continue accelerating with the pull from the winch until you are airborne.

### 6.4 First part of the tow

The first metres of altitude after takeoff are a critical phase.
Give the wing flying speed and fly actively, but calmly.
Avoid large or abrupt control movements and unnecessary use of brake.
Wait to sit in the harness if this requires a lot of movement.
If you have difficulty getting in, continue flying in an upright position.
Always prioritize:

1. control of the wing
2. correct direction of flight
3. safe altitude
4. then comfort in the harness

### 6.5 Controlled towing phase

The goal is to maintain a steady course toward the winch.
During winching, the wing can drift to one side of the ideal tow path.
A small deviation can quickly become larger if it is not corrected.
Therefore, alternate your attention between the wing and the position of the winch - the wing must fly straight toward the winch.
Correct deviations early and in a controlled manner:

- Prioritize weight shift.
- Use the brake lines if weight shift is not sufficient.
- If you already have brake applied, it may be necessary to release it on the side toward which you want to turn.

The wing is more heavily loaded during winching than during ordinary free flying.
Control input may therefore have a greater effect.
Be aware of overcorrection.

#### If you veer off course

The winch operator can reduce the pull if you veer off course.
This makes it easier to correct back toward the tow path.
Use the reduced pulling force to correct your course.
If the deviation becomes too large or you cannot correct it, the winch operator can end the tow for safety reasons.

#### Movement in the harness

Keep movements calm, especially during the first part of winching.
Do not try to sit back in the harness until you are past the first 100 critical altitude metres.
Forceful movements while trying to get into the harness can affect the wing and direction of flight.
If you cannot get into the harness easily, wait until you have: good altitude, a stable direction of flight, or disconnected from the winch line.

### 6.6 Release

When the winch operator ends the pull and you are to release:

- Maintain control of the wing and direction of flight.
- Wave with your feet or use the agreed signal to indicate that you will release.
- When you feel the force decrease, release the line.
  This is done by releasing one brake handle with one hand and pulling the release handle.
- Check that the line has actually been released.
- Continue flying only when you have control of the situation.

### 6.7 Line break at high altitude

In the event of a line break at a good altitude, first prioritize control of the wing and direction of flight.
If the line or brake parachute is still trailing behind you, consider where this will end up before you release.
If the terrain below you is suitable, just release as soon as you have control of the wing.
If you fly toward a safer area before releasing, be ready to release immediately if the line should catch on something on the ground.
As far as possible, avoid dropping line over roads, buildings, power lines, people or other activity.
Include this in your flight planning so that you are prepared for the terrain and obstacles around you.

### 6.8 Line break at low altitude

A line break shortly after takeoff is a critical situation.
When the pulling force suddenly disappears, the wing can surge forward.
If the line breaks:

- Prioritize control of the wing
- Be prepared for a surge forward and use the necessary brake to control the wing.
- Let the wing fly again when the surge forward is controlled.
- Correct the direction gently if necessary and fly safely for landing

### 6.9 After landing

If you land back at the winch area:

- Keep away from the active winch line and pilots getting ready to launch.
- Move the wing and equipment out of the launch and landing area.
- Report to the launch assistant.

The launch assistant can give you feedback on the flight and put you on the list for the next tow.

### 6.10 Emergency procedures

In an abnormal situation, the pilot must always prioritize control of the wing and a safe direction of flight.

- If you are unsure before takeoff: abort the launch or release.
- If the direction of flight is dangerously off course: correct in a controlled manner toward the winch.
- If the situation develops toward a lockout: release.
- If the line breaks: control the wing before you consider further handling of the line and dropping the line.
- If the release does not work: use an alternative release method if this is part of the approved system and training.
- After an aborted tow at low altitude: prioritize flying speed, wing control and a safe landing.

Winching is teamwork.
Follow instructions from the launch assistant and winch operator, keep track of the wing and winch throughout the winching phase, and contribute to the ground work.
If you are unsure about equipment, procedure, signal or what you should do in a situation, this must be clarified before you connect to the winch line.
