| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Aborted launch – pilot does not take off | “Point of no return” not set, poorly placed or ignored; poor run-up line for wind/terrain; technique or wing control; the wing's glide performance is poorly suited to the steepness of the launch. | Minor injury such as twisted ankle or wrist abrasions. A late abort may lead to rolling downhill and impact/fall injury. | Follow a routine and actively use the “point of no return” to abort in time. Check wing control and know your equipment. | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | Sink or collapse during departure | Lee-side conditions in west wind, rotor from the south, sink in thermic conditions, cold-air drainage, or insufficient takeoff speed leaving the wing under-pressurised. | Emergency landing or crash landing in trees/terrain. | Understand local conditions, clear trees below launch and maintain good wing control. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | Takeoff in unsuitable conditions | Reluctance to go down again; pressure from spectators, sheep, group dynamics, cameras or personal condition. Weather changes may go unnoticed. | Critical situations in flight and potentially difficult landing conditions. | Build sound attitudes and knowledge of risk assessment, meteorology and local conditions. | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | Landing in trees | Ridge soaring close to terrain, for example in marginal lift (thermal, but especially mechanical), or flying in conditions too advanced for the pilot's competence. | Injury to pilot and wing. | Do not overestimate your skills or the conditions. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 5 | Collapse over land and reserve deployment | Incorrect low-altitude manoeuvre practice, for example during training. | Emergency/crash landing; hazards include power lines, river and water at landing. | Keep a generous flight-plan margin suited to competence. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 6 | Airspace conflict | Skydiving aircraft or tow planes may have flight paths close to Gjelle. | Uncomfortable proximity; worst case collision if pilots are unaware and do not use visibility tools. | Use SafeSky or similar and share local airspace activity. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 7 | Misjudging actual wind strength | Terrain features and the location of the weather station. | Takeoff in rotor. | Check nearby weather stations to understand mean wind and direction. | 1 | 2 | 2 |
