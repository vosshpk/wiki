# Risk assessment: Tverrberg

| Field | Information |
|---|---|
| Club | Voss Hang- og Paragliderklubb |
| Site | Tverrberg |
| Prepared by | Malene Hetle Hole |
| Date in source | 11.09.2026 |

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Airspace conflict with aircraft | Launch and possible flight paths are close to Bømoen airport. | Uncomfortable proximity/turbulence; worst case collision between paraglider and skydiving aircraft, potentially fatal. | Emphasise visibility and routines. Communicate across air-sport clubs, inform members and share recommended flight paths. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 2 | Misjudging actual wind – takeoff in rotor | Unfavourable wind against terrain shape. Both a paraglider launch and ramp are used; each requires wing control and experience. | Sink/collapse and crash landing in terrain. | Check nearby weather stations for mean wind/direction. Assess terrain and slope; sharp edges create rotor. | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 | Sink after launch | Insufficient takeoff speed, technique or misjudged wind/tailwind; late abort. | Aborted launch/landing in terrain just after takeoff, injuring pilot/wing. | Keep margins, know equipment/skills, assess local terrain and avoid suboptimal wind direction. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | Landing in trees/crash | Collapse in turbulence while soaring near terrain, especially mechanical lift. | Emergency/crash landing with serious injury to pilot and equipment. | Adapt each flight plan to skills and conditions. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind | The launch is often used in west wind. Sea breeze and terrain lift may create strong wind. Desire to fly/effort invested may obscure the weather; fronts may rapidly change wind and bring rain. | Collapse/sink, discomfort or danger; possible secondary emergency/crash landing. | Use weather stations and local information; post signs at launch. Forecast/measurement differences or forecasts of changing weather indicate unpredictability. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 6 | Emergency landing | Poor flight plan, major altitude loss during manoeuvres, or inadequate assessment/change in weather. | Secondary injury from a poor landing. | Map alternative landing sites and clarify decision altitude. | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 7 | Misjudged top landing | Wind may create mechanical turbulence. Overestimating skills or a spontaneous decision to land. | Injury from a poor landing or tree landing. | Practise top-landing plans and technique at suitable sites with generous margins. Avoid spontaneous decisions. In an emergency, choose the landing area with the greatest margin below launch. | 1 | 2 | 2 |
