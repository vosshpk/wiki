| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Stress at launch | Tourists, spectators and sheep; group dynamics and cameras. | Stress reduces focus and may lead to poor assessment or launch technique. | Help one another, allow for daily condition and give each other time for equipment checks. | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | Front collapse/collapse during takeoff | Changing conditions, uneven acceleration or unloading over rough ground, trees, bushes and tall grass; poor line choice can unload the wing. | Landing/crash in terrain immediately after launch, injuring pilot and/or wing. | Smooth vegetation and focus training on terrain and line choice. | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 3 | Insufficient takeoff speed | Technique or misjudgement of wind strength/tailwind. | Aborted launch or landing in terrain just after launch, injuring pilot and/or wing. | Maintain margins, know equipment and skills, and encourage ground handling. | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 4 | Sink or collapse after launch | Lee-side conditions in east/west crosswind, e.g. sea-breeze or sink in thermic conditions. Common later in the day when flying conditions are good. | Late abort/failed launch may cause minor injury to pilot and/or wing. | Assess conditions and local terrain. Build a culture of avoiding launches in suboptimal wind directions. | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind or rain | Strong desire to fly and reluctance to descend can obscure the weather picture. Convergence, wave conditions or fronts may cause rapid changes. | Collapse or sink; discomfort or danger. | Use weather stations and local information; post signs at launch. Differences between forecast and measured wind, or forecasts of changing wind/weather, indicate unpredictability. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 6 | Flying in strong thermals, especially in spring/early progression | Easy access and high activity. A high-performance wing beyond the pilot's experience makes turbulence/collapse harder to handle; lack of SIV training/understanding. | In-flight collapse, possibly reserve deployment. | Use suitable equipment with margins and progression for thermal flying. Arrange supervised introduction to lift for PP2/PP3 pilots. | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 7 | Gondola cable | Loss of control while crossing the cable, e.g. collapse in turbulence or insufficient clearance. Focus on route planning/soaring may hide obstacles and altitude loss. | Collision with cable/cabin and crash landing. | Cross the cable with the greatest possible clearance and height. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 8 | Landing in trees/crash | Collapse in turbulence while soaring close to terrain, especially mechanical lift; also proxy flying on SPG. | Emergency/crash landing with serious injury to pilot and equipment. | Adapt each flight plan to skills and conditions. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 9 | Losing loose objects | Inadequate securing and awareness while focusing on the flight plan. | Damage to houses or third parties. | Regularly remind pilots to secure items, especially skis in winter and cameras/other equipment year-round. | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 10 | Canopy collision | No prior shared flight plan; different speeds for SPG, PG and skydiving. Relevant during busy periods/events. | Reserve deployment or crash landing injuring one or two pilots; third-party injury over buildings. | Give extra information during busy activity/events and clearly communicate rules. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 11 | Reserve deployment over water | Manoeuvre practice, acro or SIV. | Injury during emergency landing; drowning risk if rescue boat is not staffed or it is winter. | Have two rescue boats in season and staff a boat during courses. | 1 | 3 | 2 |
