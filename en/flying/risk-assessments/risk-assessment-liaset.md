## Risk assessment

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Aborted launch: slip/trip | It may lead to, or result from, a frontal or asymmetric collapse. Poor wing control combined with failure to set the “point of no return” and assess the run-up line and distance for the tow and acceleration. Misjudged weather or pilot skills may also contribute. Typical when launching in crosswind, light wind or rotor. | Fall and muscle/joint injury, typically cuts, impact injury or sprain. | Progression course focused on launch technique and good wing control through ground handling. Encourage wing-control practice throughout a flying career. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 2 | Sink or collapse after launch | Lee-side turbulence in crosswind, sea breeze, thermic sink or evening cold-air drainage. Common late in the day when conditions are good. Sink may also occur if the pilot sits down abruptly before full airspeed. | Near miss with little terrain clearance, or emergency landing injuring pilot/wing. Possible compression fracture from sitting abruptly in the harness. | Assess local conditions and terrain. Avoid launching in suboptimal wind directions. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 3 | Tree snag/landing – tall spruce below takeoff | Misjudged weather. After launch this may happen with unsuitable wind direction or strength and too little margin to regain control and accelerate well. Low speed after the “point of no return” may also result from insufficient running, wind or a braked takeoff. | Fall and muscle/joint injury, cuts, impact, back or suspension injury. | Remove trees and bushes. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Emergency landing | Equipment fault such as cravat/tension knot, altitude loss during manoeuvre or inadequate assessment/change in weather. | Secondary injury from a poor landing. | Map alternative landing sites and clarify decision altitude. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind/rain | Strong desire to fly and time/logistics invested may obscure the weather picture. Convergence, wave conditions or fronts may change rapidly. | A critical in-flight situation involving collapse or parachutal stall. Even if the flight goes well, losing control or being in danger may be an uncomfortable experience. A secondary consequence may be an emergency or crash landing. | Use weather stations and available information about local weather phenomena; post signs at launch. A difference between forecast and recorded wind indicates unpredictability. The same applies when a forecast reports different weather types, wind directions and wind strengths through the day. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 6 | Injury during top landing | Misjudged conditions, wind strength, terrain or skill. | Minor to serious pilot injury, commonly ankle or compression fracture; possible injury to a third party or damage to property. | Plan the flight, account for daily condition and leave room to abort the approach. Practise top landings repeatedly at suitable sites. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 7 | Power lines and other cables | Poor mapping/flight planning may reduce obstacle clearance. Misjudged weather or fixation on another event may reduce awareness. | Serious pilot injury and possible third-party property damage. | Establish a safety zone and information map. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 8 | Canopy collision (ridge/thermal and PG/SPG approach) | Conflict near takeoff or ridge/thermal; poor communication at launch and failure to complete the five-point check that the airspace is clear, or loss of awareness/focus in flight. | Serious injury to two people. | Plan flights, follow right-of-way rules and account for fitness. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
