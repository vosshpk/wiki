## Risk assessment

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Aborted launch: slip/trip at takeoff. | May lead to, or be caused by, a frontal/asymmetric collapse. Comes from poor wing control in connection with not setting the point of no return, assessing the line and distance for tow and acceleration. Another cause may be misjudged weather conditions and/or one's own skills. Typical when launching in crosswind, light wind or rotor. | Fall, injury to muscles and joints. Typically cuts, impact injury, sprain. | Progression course focused on launch technique; good wing control is achieved by focusing on personal skills in ground handling. Encourage wing-control training throughout the flying career. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 2 | Sink or collapse after launch | As a result of turbulence on the lee side in crosswind. E.g. in sea breeze or sink area in thermic conditions or cold-air drainage in the evening. This is a very common problem that occurs later in the day when flying conditions are good. Sink can also occur after launch if full airspeed is not reached and one jumps out/sits down abruptly. | Possible near miss with just enough clearance from terrain. Alternatively, an emergency landing with injury to pilot and/or wing. A compression fracture if one sits abruptly in the harness too early | Focus on assessing conditions and the influence of local terrain formations. Build attitudes against launching in sub. optimal wind direction. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 3 | Tree snag/landing - tall spruce trees below takeoff | Misjudged weather conditions. This may happen after takeoff if wind direction or strength is not optimal, combined with too little margin to regain control and accelerate well. Another cause of low speed after the point of no return is insufficient running, wind or takeoff with brakes. | Fall, injury to muscles and joints. Typically cuts, impact injury, back injury. Suspension injury. | Removal of trees and bushes. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Emergency landing | Equipment fault such as cravat/tension knot, or major altitude loss while performing manoeuvres, inadequate assessment of weather conditions or weather changes during flight. | Secondary injury from a poor landing. | Mapping alternative landing sites and making pilots aware of the minimum decision altitude. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind, rain. | Can happen with a strong desire to fly, reinforced by the time and logistics spent getting to launch. This can mean that one does not see the whole picture regarding forecast and conditions. Convergence lift, wave conditions, weather fronts that cause rapid changes. | Critical situation in the air regarding collapse or parachutal stall. If the flight goes well, it can still be an unpleasant experience to lose control or be in danger. | Weather stations, available information about local weather phenomena. Sign at launch. A difference between forecast and recorded wind is a sign of unpredictability. The same is a forecast reporting different types of weather, wind directions and wind strength through the day. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 6 | Injury at top landing. | Misjudged conditions, wind strength, terrain and/or skills. | Pilot injury, minor to serious, typically ankle or compression fracture. Also risk of injury to a third party, material and person. | Flight plan, awareness of daily condition and margin to abort the approach attempt. Repeated practice at suitable sites for top landing. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 7 | Power lines and other cables | Lack of mapping in the flight plan. This can reduce margins and clearance from obstacles if weather conditions are misjudged. Alternatively, hyperfocus on another problem or event while flying can cause loss of overview. | Serious pilot injury, and potentially material damage to a third party. | Safety zone and information map | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 8 | Canopy collision (ridge/thermal and PG/SPG approach) | Conflict between pilots in the air around takeoff, possibly between pilots on the ridge/in a thermal. Inadequate communication at launch and five-point check (clear airspace), as well as loss of overview and focus during flight. | Serious injury to two people. | Flight plan, right-of-way rules and fitness are key areas of focus. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
