## Risk assessment

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Pilot gets stuck in the ski lift (T-bar on the way up) | Lack of equipment checks and overview. | Injury to pilot and equipment, and lift stoppage. | Equipment check. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 2 | Takeoff with malfunction: tension knot, cravat, line-over | In Myrkdalen, where the terrain is gentle and it is easy to get many runs, this can lead to less focus on equipment checks. There may also be insufficient preparation and checking of the wing before the point of no return. | One does not take off and stops below takeoff. Emergency landing in the terrain. | Equipment check. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 3 | Does not get lift/airspeed before the planned takeoff point | Tailwind, low slope angle, wet snow that slows the skis. | Stops in steeper terrain below launch. | Assess weather conditions together with terrain, snow conditions, wing type and size, and type of skis and glide. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Cannot stop at the point of no return | Skiing skills or stopping technique are not good enough, and mental capacity is therefore lower. | Takeoff with a malfunction or crash landing below launch. | Follow-up on courses, and focus on ski technique. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Collision with skiers | When one gets off the lift and crosses the piste on skis, on the way towards the piste. In a takeoff situation skiers can come in from the side. The same can happen while riding, or skiers may stand below steeper edges. It may be a surprise if one is speedriding. The same applies on landing. | Collision at high speed, injury to pilot, skier and equipment. | Focus on the five-point check and what the last point about airspace entails. We focus on this during courses and remind pilots about it regularly throughout the season. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> |
| 5 | Pilot in piste/lift corridor | Too little margin in the flight plan. | Collision at high speed, injury to pilot, skier and equipment. | Safety zone: minimum 20 m distance in all directions from pistes and ski lifts. Takeoff direction parallel to the piste and ski lift. Take wind direction and possible crosswind into account to avoid drifting over the piste and lift. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 6 | Crash in creek gully while flying or skiing | Flight plan with little margin, flat light, changed formations through the season. | Collision at high speed, injury to pilot and equipment. | Flying close to terrain entails greater risk. This also involves experience, skills, ability to plan and assess many factors, as well as mental capacity while flying. Much of the same applies to skiing in the terrain. Contribute to awareness and work to increase understanding of this. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 7 | Release of a slab on a steep slope (avalanche) | Unstable snow conditions and lack of understanding and competence about this. Travelling outside the ski area is at one's own risk. | It can release larger sections, carry skiers down the terrain and possibly put several people in the terrain below at risk. | During the basic course, we focus especially on this: avalanche terrain, runout zone and avalanche forecast. If in doubt, one should not enter steep terrain. Encourage pilots to seek continuous knowledge about avalanches and assessment. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 8 | Loses skis in connection with flying | Missing retention strap. | The skis at high speed can cause injury to other people or buildings. Possibly also the skier themselves if this causes a fall. | Reminder about safety in the ski centre, review of regulations and practical measures on courses. A retention strap on tech bindings or a locking mechanism on the binding is mandatory. Also be especially aware of flying over people and buildings, and avoid it as much as possible. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 9 | Collision with an obstacle on landing | Approach pattern with too little margin, changing wind conditions along the way. Target fixation. | Collision at high speed, injury to pilot and material damage. | Go through the flight plan for each flight and assess it against the weather conditions. Focus on starting the approach planning early in the flight, looking far ahead and focusing on a large open area. Practise precision landing throughout the progression. Assess the landing point in relation to terrain slope, formations and obstacles, and give oneself a good margin for this so there is room for misjudgement. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 10 | Barrel rolls/advanced manoeuvres at low altitude | Myrkdalen does not provide sufficient height to practise this safely if one has small wings. | Crash at high speed and potentially serious personal injury. | Encourage practising wing control while riding, and generally flying different lines without manoeuvres that cause major altitude loss. If one needs more challenge, one can practise controlled riding down steeper terrain and a smaller turn radius. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 11 | Pilot crashes on skis on the way to takeoff or on the way home after the day is over. | Poor skiing skills, speed beyond one's ability. Tired after a long day or wanting to ski fast. | Injury to oneself or others. | Focus on self-reflection about daily condition and skills. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 12 | Wind increase while flying/kiting | Wave conditions: the optimal wind direction in Myrkdalen is south, southeast. At the wind speed for kiting, this causes wave conditions. Too little margin in the flight plan in relation to the forecast and potential changes. | Dragging, rotor, collapse and crash. Injury to pilot. | Flying in wave conditions entails greater risk. Take time to check the weather and wind along the way. Map the maximum gusts and trend during the day. Plan based on experience, skills, equipment and terrain so one is always prepared to handle an increase in wind. In stronger wind, there is always an advantage to having more terrain behind, as this gives a greater opportunity to stop and take control of the wing before any rotor. The same applies when flying: it is safer to keep a distance from the strongest lift zone. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> |
