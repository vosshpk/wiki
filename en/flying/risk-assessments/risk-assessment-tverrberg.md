## Risk assessment

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Airspace conflict aircraft | The launch and possible flight paths are near Bømoen airport. | Unpleasant events during close passing. Such as uncertainty and possible turbulence. In the worst case, collision between paraglider and skydiving aircraft causing a fatal accident with several involved. | Focus on visibility in airspace and routines about it. Communication between the different air-sport clubs and information to members about activities. Share information about recommended flight paths for all parties. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 2 | Misjudging actual wind strength - takeoff in rotor | Unfavourable wind conditions against the terrain shape. Possible selection of an unfavourable takeoff site since there is both a paraglider launch and a ramp. Both launches require good wing control and experience assessing conditions. | Sink after launch, or collapse and crash landing in the terrain. | Check nearby weather stations to get a good picture of actual mean wind strength and direction. Assess terrain shape and steepness. Sharp edges in the terrain create rotor. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> |
| 3 | Sink after launch | Too low takeoff speed. Technique and/or misjudgement of conditions regarding wind speed or tailwind. Late aborted start. | Aborted launch/landing in terrain just after launch and thus injury to pilot and/or wing. | Increase margin in launch conditions, know own equipment and skills. Focus on assessing conditions and the influence of local terrain formations. Build attitudes against using the launch in sub.optimal wind direction. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Tree landing/crash | Collapse due to turbulence at launch, or during ridge soaring close to terrain, e.g. at a marginal ridge (thermic, but especially mechanical). | Emergency landing or crash in the terrain and further damage and serious injuries to pilot with equipment. | Focus on a flight plan for each flight adapted to skills and conditions. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind | This launch is often used in west wind, and with sea breeze there can be strong wind. In addition to lift zone from the terrain. Can happen with a strong desire to fly, reinforced by the time and logistics spent getting to launch. This can mean one does not see the whole picture regarding forecast and conditions. In addition, weather fronts can cause rapid changes. Such as rain and increased/changed wind strength and direction. | Critical situation in the air regarding collapse or parachutal stall. If the flight goes well, it can still be an unpleasant experience to lose control or be in danger. Secondary consequence is emergency landing/crash landing. | Weather stations, available information about local weather phenomena. Sign at launch. A difference between forecast and recorded wind is a sign of unpredictability. The same is a forecast reporting different weather types, wind directions and wind strength through the day. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 6 | Emergency landing | Lack in flight plan, major altitude loss while performing manoeuvres, inadequate assessment of weather conditions or weather changes during flight. | Secondary injury due to poor landing. | Mapping alternative landing sites and making pilots aware of the minimum decision altitude. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 7 | Miscalculated top landing | Challenging conditions, if there is wind it can cause mechanical turbulence. Overestimating skills since top landing requires experience and skills suited to the conditions. Also, a spontaneous decision to land due to motivation to land can lead to miscalculations. | Injury from poor landing, possibly tree landing | Practise a good plan and technique for top landing at suitable places with good margin. Avoid spontaneous decision to land. In an emergency, choose landings with the greatest possible margin that are below launch. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
