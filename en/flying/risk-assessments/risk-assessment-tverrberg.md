## Risk assessment

| No. | Event/situation | Cause | Risk/consequence | Mitigation | Likelihood | Consequence | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Airspace conflict with aircraft | Launch and possible flight paths are close to Bømoen airport. | Uncomfortable close encounters, such as uncertainty and possible turbulence. In the worst case, a paraglider and skydiving aircraft could collide, causing a fatal accident involving several people. | Emphasise visibility and routines. Communicate across air-sport clubs, inform members and share recommended flight paths. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 2 | Misjudging actual wind – takeoff in rotor | Unfavourable wind conditions against the terrain shape. There is a possibility of choosing an unsuitable takeoff site because both a paraglider launch and a ramp are available. Both require good wing control and experience assessing conditions. | Sink/collapse and crash landing in terrain. | Check nearby weather stations for mean wind/direction. Assess terrain and slope; sharp edges create rotor. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> |
| 3 | Sink after launch | Insufficient takeoff speed, technique or misjudged wind/tailwind; late abort. | Aborted launch/landing in terrain just after takeoff, injuring pilot/wing. | Keep margins, know equipment/skills, assess local terrain and avoid suboptimal wind direction. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 4 | Landing in trees/crash | Collapse in turbulence at launch or while soaring near terrain, for example in thermic lift but especially on a marginal mechanical ridge. | Emergency/crash landing with serious injury to pilot and equipment. | Adapt each flight plan to skills and conditions. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--3">3</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 5 | Flying in strong wind | The launch is often used in west wind. Sea breeze and terrain lift may create strong wind. Desire to fly and time/logistics invested may obscure the weather; fronts may rapidly change wind or bring rain. | A critical in-flight situation involving collapse or parachutal stall. Even if the flight goes well, losing control or being in danger may be an uncomfortable experience. A secondary consequence may be an emergency or crash landing. | Use weather stations and available information about local weather phenomena; post signs at launch. A difference between forecast and recorded wind indicates unpredictability. The same applies when a forecast reports different weather types, wind directions and wind strengths through the day. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 6 | Emergency landing | Poor flight plan, major altitude loss during manoeuvres, or inadequate assessment/change in weather. | Secondary injury from a poor landing. | Map alternative landing sites and clarify decision altitude. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
| 7 | Misjudged top landing | Wind may create mechanical turbulence. Top landing requires experience and skills suited to the conditions. Overestimating skills or a spontaneous decision to land due to motivation may lead to miscalculation. | Injury from a poor landing or tree landing. | Practise top-landing plans and technique at suitable sites with generous margins. Avoid spontaneous decisions. In an emergency, choose the landing area with the greatest margin below launch. | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--1">1</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> | <span class="voss-ros-risk-level voss-ros-risk-level--2">2</span> |
