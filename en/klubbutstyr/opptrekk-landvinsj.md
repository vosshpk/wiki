# Ground winch towing procedures

**Source document:** Revised towing procedures for the DaWinchi ground winch, Voss HPK, dated 12 September 2026.

> **Status:** The procedure says it must be approved by NLF/HPS before towing activity begins.
> Confirm its approval, revision status and local conditions with the club before use.
> This Markdown translation is not an independent approval.

## Introduction

### 1 Approval and current procedure

This procedure applies to towing with the DaWinchi ground winch operated by Voss HPK.
NLF/HPS must approve the procedure before towing activity begins.
All pilots, operators and assistants taking part must know the parts of the procedure that apply to their roles.
The procedure must have a revision number and date.
Changes to equipment, the winch, local conditions or working methods must prompt an assessment of whether the procedure needs to be updated and approved again.

### 1.1 Towing site and area

Sites used regularly for towing must be approved for this purpose by NLF/HPS.
The sites used at Voss are:

- Lotatreet
- Istad
- Dyrvedalen

Before the activity, the person responsible for towing must check that:

- landowner permission has been clarified;
- the NOTAM is valid for the site, time and planned altitude;
- launch and landing areas are suitable and clear of obstacles;
- the towing direction and line route provide sufficient clearance from roads, buildings, power lines, people and other obstacles; and
- alternative landing options are available in the event of a line break or aborted winch launch.

### 1.2 Flying and movement in the towing area

During active winching, the tow path and the area around the line must be kept clear of unauthorized people, vehicles and other activity.
Other pilots must keep well clear of the active tow line and must not cross the tow path while a tow is in progress.
Do not begin another tow until the airspace, launch area and line area are clear.
If needed, secure the area with ground assistants, signs or other suitable markings.

The person responsible for the activity must ensure that a valid NOTAM is in place for the towing site and the period of operation.

## 2 Qualifications and roles during towing

For towing, a pilot must hold a paragliding towing qualification (OTP) or be training to obtain one.
A regular PP2–PP5 rating alone is not sufficient to be towed.
Beginners may nevertheless be taught using towing from their first day as part of organized training.

| Role | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Pilot being towed | OTP or undergoing OTP training |
| OTP | At least 10 approved tows, plus skill, knowledge and air-sports conduct requirements |
| Winch / towing-device operator | Qualification for the specific device |
| Operator in training | At least 10 successful tows under supervision |
| PIO – paragliding towing instructor | PI + OTP + at least 20 tows + approved instruction under supervision |
| Person responsible for the activity | Named person holding a towing qualification |
| Launch assistant / ground crew | Knowledge and training needed for the task under the local procedure |

### 2.1 Pilot – OTP

OTP is additional training for paragliding towing.
The pilot must be able to protect their own safety and that of others during towing, and must master:

- the correct release procedure;
- the correct position relative to the towing device;
- speed and direction corrections; and
- handling rapid changes in climb rate and position caused by wind and thermals.

The pilot must also understand the forces acting during the different phases of a tow, the tow line, weak link, release system, function and limitations of the towing device, and the applicable procedures, signals and emergency procedures.

To obtain OTP, the pilot must complete at least 10 approved tows.

The OTP qualification is renewed together with the regular rating.
To maintain towing proficiency, the minimum requirement is 5 tows during the previous 60 months.

### 2.2 Towing-device operator

The winch or towing-device operator must hold a qualification for the specific device.
This qualification is tied to the towing device itself and must not be confused with the pilot's OTP qualification.

Operator training must:

- be provided by a person who is themselves trained on the device;
- be documented in writing; and
- include at least 10 successful tows under supervision and instruction before a qualification can be issued.

An operator with at least 50 successful tows using another towing device may operate a new type of device.

### 2.3 Paragliding towing instructor – PIO

OTP training and proficiency checks must be conducted by a person with the required instructor qualifications.
PIO, paragliding towing instructor, is additional training for instructors.

To become a PIO, a candidate must:

1. hold a paragliding instructor qualification (PI);
2. hold OTP;
3. have completed at least 20 tows; and
4. have instructed during an OTP check under the supervision of a PIO and been approved.

### 2.4 Person responsible for towing activity

A named person holding a towing qualification must be responsible for the operation.
The towing-device operator must also ensure that ground assistants have the knowledge needed to perform their tasks safely.

This means a launch assistant or other helper does not necessarily need a separate “launch-assistant certificate,” but must be instructed and have the necessary knowledge for the task they perform.

### 2.5 Ground crew and launch

There must be enough ground crew to keep unauthorized people at a safe distance from the activity.
All pilots who are to be towed must familiarize themselves with the approved towing procedure before flying.

## 3 Local activity – DaWinchi ground winch

Everyone using this type of winch must receive sufficient training from the club's designated towing leads.
At present, Lars Haukom, Morten Knapstad and Kjell Svanes have the most experience with the club's winch and local towing sites, and hold operator and towing-instructor qualifications.
One of them must therefore be responsible for the activity.
The responsible group may be expanded gradually as more people gain the same qualifications.

### 3.1 Training new operators, OTP pilots and instructors

Training may only take place when a local towing instructor or local winch operator is present.
OTP training must be conducted by a towing instructor, and formal course registration must be in place.

A towing instructor may train a new winch operator if an experienced OTP pilot is flying.
The recommended local guideline is an OTP pilot with at least 50 tows.
Do not have a newly qualified OTP pilot flying while the operator is also in training.
This gives both trainees the best learning conditions.

As a general recommendation, first join as a ground assistant and gradually become familiar with the components, checklists and routines.
As you gain a good overview and the correct roles are in place, you can gradually take on more responsibility.
It helps if a prospective winch operator already has experience being towed as an OTP pilot.
Help check and fit the equipment for the pilot who is flying.
After that, it is natural to carry out the first tow.
If shorter flights are used to train an operator, an experienced OTP pilot with good mental capacity and wing control must fly.

### 3.2 Activity planning

- Confirm who is participating and who has overall responsibility.
  Make sure the required qualifications and responsible roles are in place.
- Always check the forecast.
  Days with crosswind or wind speeds above 5 m/s should be avoided.
- Check with all landowners at the planned site for each activity.
- Check for a NOTAM 24 hours before use, and no later than 16:00 the day before.
  If appropriate, call the skydiving club and gliding club on the day.
  They can see us in the SafeSky app.
  Enter the call sign “Paraglider winch to .... m”.
- Put the winch on charge the evening before.
  Charging takes 4–5 hours.
  Do not charge it immediately before use.
- Load the car with the winch equipment and first-aid kit.

### 3.3 Equipment requirements and daily check

Before the first tow of the day, the operator and pilot are responsible for checking the equipment and safety systems.
Among other things, check that:

- the pilot's release system is correctly installed and works;
- the release is easily accessible during every phase of the tow;
- the release has been inspected and function-tested;
- the winch has a working emergency release or line cutter;
- the line, line guide, attachment and drum have no visible damage;
- any weak link is suitable for the system in use; and
- radio communication and the remote control work, where used.

The safety system requires an effective, easily accessible release that the pilot can activate throughout the tow, and an emergency release or line cutter on the towing device.
Releases must be inspected and tested daily.

### 3.4 Accident and emergency response

First-aid equipment must be available at the launch area before activity begins.

In the event of an accident:

1. Stop towing activity.
2. Secure the area and remove any danger from the line, winch or traffic.
3. Provide necessary first aid.
4. Call 113 in the event of serious injury or danger to life.
5. Give the location, type of incident and state that it involves paragliding/towing.
6. Notify the Voss HPK Technical Director (Fagleg leiar).
7. Follow the club's action and notification plan.
8. Report the incident under the current NLF/HPS procedures.

### 3.5 Preparing the winch

They show the winch attached to the tow hitch, the control unit, battery and antenna.

![DaWinchi mounted on a car and tow hitch](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vosshpk/wiki/main/assets/landvinsj-transport.jpg "DaWinchi mounted on a car")

- All users of this unit need the correct towing qualifications.
  This applies to the winch operator, pilot or towing student, and towing instructor.
- Treat the line with great respect when using DaWinchi.
  Do not touch any moving parts.
- Reassess the forecast and actual weather.
  Crosswind or wind speeds above 5 m/s should be avoided.
- Apply the car's parking brake before towing.
- Always keep a professional line cutter within reach during towing, both at the winch and in the cockpit.
- Use DaWinchi at your own risk.
  Do not allow anyone to operate it unless they are a certified and experienced towing operator.
- Keep first-aid equipment at the towing launch.
- Do not use a pickup bed or anything protruding from it, as the winch moves upward during a tow.

### 3.6 Technical check

Before and after use, make sure the control box is set to INT and the INT slider is at 0, so it does not start spinning by itself during setup.

![DaWinchi control box and components](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vosshpk/wiki/main/assets/landvinsj-kontrollboks.jpg "Control box and components")

- INT must be the setting at startup and shutdown.
  The INT slider must be to the left.
- Tape the line or secure it by another method when testing.
- Start and stop slowly when winding in during use.
  At the end of the day, the line should be wound in firmly at full speed.
  When winding in the line, stop well in advance.
  Operators in training should practise this.
- Check for wear on the loop.
  Repair it when it becomes frayed.
  Keep a knife and splicing materials in the box.
- Keep a knife by the motor for cutting the line.
- Always tension the line before connecting it to the pilot.
- Maintain the battery when the winch is not in use.

### 3.7 Setup

1. Place the control box on the right and the battery on the left.
2. Connect the antenna to the five-pin socket on top of the control box.
3. Put the receiver on the antenna's telescopic pole and extend it.
   Secure the pole in the ground.
4. Insert the red key and make sure it points to “OFF”.
   Set the on/off switch to “0”.
5. Open the box.
   Move the small switch to the left (INT).
   The spool is now driven locally.
6. Connect the battery to the control box.
7. Check the control voltage on the box.
   It should rise; when it slows at around 60–70 V, the main power can be switched on with the key.
   Switching it on too early may damage the controller.
   The displayed voltage should then be around 80–100 V, depending on battery type and charge.
8. Attach the winch mount to the tow hitch.
   Tighten the wing nuts and check that it can swivel.
   Use grease or oil to improve movement.
9. Slide the DaWinchi onto the mount, insert the large and small bolts, and secure them.
10. Make sure the sleeve is centered and does not make a noise when you rotate the spool by hand.
11. Attach the line guide and tighten the screws and wing nut.
    The line should build up in the middle of the spool.
    Adjust it after the first few tows if necessary.
    The line must be mounted in the center.
    To adjust the line guide, loosen the bolt and move the guide so the line is centered.
12. Insert the large motor plug on the side of the controller and the six-pin control plug.
13. Turn on the controller by pressing the black button to “1”.
    DaWinchi is now ready to spin.
14. Use the throttle slider marked “INT” to rotate the spool.
    Tape the line to the side of the spool and test at full speed.
16. Switch to RC to alternate between the green and orange remote controls.
17. Run the line from the spool through the pulley.
    Attach the brake parachute with the carabiner.
    Use the retrieval vehicle to pull the line out slowly for takeoff.
    DaWinchi is now ready for towing.

## 4 Operator instructions – winching with a ground operator

- Check the radio signal at launch by holding the line (do not wrap it around your hand) and applying a little throttle.
- Do not jerk the line, as this may cause tangles.
- Attach the line to the pilot and complete the equipment check with a second check.
- Build up light tension in the line and let the pilot's corresponding forward movement bring the wing up.
- When the wing is stable and the pilot is in control, increase throttle according to wind strength.
- Use caution with the throttle until the pilot has reached a safe height, at least 30–50 metres above the ground.
  Monitor the angle of attack throughout the tow, and control the wing's pitch and movement by adjusting the pull with the throttle.
- When the pilot reaches the desired height, release the throttle.
  The controller will automatically reduce line pull gradually so the pilot can release.
- Remember that there is a 3-second delay after releasing the throttle.

Maintain a steady speed while winding in the line.
Avoid stopping and starting during retrieval, as this may cause tangles.
Do not wind in the line without resistance/tension.

### 4.1 Emergency procedures – winch operator

In an abnormal situation, the winch operator must prioritize reducing or removing the pulling force.

**Aborted launch**

- Release the throttle/pull immediately.
- Check that the line goes slack.
- Do not begin another tow until the cause has been clarified.

**Pilot veers off course**

- Reduce the pull to give the pilot a better chance to correct course.
- If the deviation increases or a lockout risk arises, terminate the pull.

**Risk of lockout**

- Remove the pulling force immediately.
- Use the emergency release or line cutter if the line does not release.

**Loss of radio or remote-control signal**

- Stop or reduce the pull if the tow can no longer be controlled.
- During self-winching, the pilot must follow the separate signal-loss emergency procedure.

**Line or drum jams**

- Stop the winch.
- If the pilot is still connected and the line cannot be slackened, use the emergency release or line cutter.

**Technical fault**

- Terminate the tow.
- Do not use the winch again until the fault has been identified and corrected.

## 5 Self-winching

During self-winching, the pilot has full control of the winching and is therefore responsible for conducting it safely.
Self-winching may only be carried out if the pilot is qualified both as a pilot being towed and to operate the specific towing device.

During self-winching, the pilot is both the pilot and the person controlling the towing device.
The pilot must therefore be particularly familiar with:

- the remote control and emergency shutdown;
- loss of radio signal;
- delay in adjusting the pulling force;
- line and brake-parachute checks;
- aborted launches;
- alternative landing areas; and
- emergency release and line cutting.

The safety system requires both a qualification for the person being towed and a qualification for the specific towing device held by its operator.
At Voss HPK, self-winching may only be carried out after approval and training from one of the club's local towing instructors.

Important points:

- Do not turn on the remote control until the wing is fully inflated.
  Then build light line tension, take off and select the desired climb rate with the throttle.
- Attach the remote-control strap to your preferred hand.
  Put your wrist through the brake loop and point the remote control at the antenna.
  Steer the wing by moving your arm up and down.
- Use caution with the throttle in strong winds.
- If you abort the launch, land and walk back to the launch with the line to avoid tangles.
- Keep the remote control pointed at the antenna.
- If the connection is interrupted, the remote control will continue with the current throttle setting when the connection returns.
  Therefore, keep the throttle low until the connection is restored.
- If the remote control is switched off, the signal stops and the winch stops pulling.
- At the end of the tow, release the throttle and wait until the controller has reduced line pull sufficiently before releasing.
- Keep an eye on the brake parachute while the line is winding in.
- Turn off the remote control and continue flying.

## 6 Pilot instructions for paragliding (PG) winching

Winching is an effective way to get many flights.
Safe and efficient winching requires good cooperation between the pilot, launch assistant and winch operator.
The pilot is always responsible for their own flying and equipment.

- The paraglider must be approved by its manufacturer for towing and be free of damage.
- The harness must be undamaged and have at least one reserve parachute repacked within the last 12 months.
- An approved release must be used, and it must be undamaged.

### 6.1 Before winching

Report to the launch assistant when you arrive and put your name on the queue list.

- Prepare your wing and equipment while you wait.
- Help with the line, equipment and other preparations.
- Be ready when it is your turn.

Winching is teamwork – everyone must contribute.

### 6.2 Pre-launch check

Before connecting to the winch line, check:

- Perform the five-point check and make sure no lines are caught on the harness, shoes or similar items.
- The wing and harness are correctly connected.
- All buckles are closed and locked.
- The helmet strap is fastened.
- The release system is correctly installed and works.
- The weak link has the correct rating.

For paragliding, the weak link must be rated at 75% of the all-up weight (pilot + equipment).

The launch assistant may help with the check, but the pilot is responsible for ensuring that the equipment is correct and ready before launch.

### 6.3 Launch

When you, the launch assistant and winch operator are ready, the command “Winch on” is given.

As the line tightens:

- Follow the line's pull calmly by moving forward.
- Do not resist the pull, but do not run so that the line goes slack.
- Prioritize control of the wing and direction.
- Give the wing full flying speed, correcting on only one side at a time if needed.
- Look forward as soon as the wing is balanced above your head.
- Continue accelerating with the winch pull until airborne.

### 6.4 First part of the tow

The first few metres of altitude after takeoff are a critical phase.

Give the wing flying speed and fly actively but calmly.
Avoid large or sudden control inputs and unnecessary brake use.
Wait to sit back in the harness if doing so requires a lot of movement.
If you have trouble getting in, continue flying upright.

Always prioritize:

1. control of the wing;
2. correct flying direction;
3. safe altitude; and
4. comfort in the harness.

### 6.5 Controlled towing phase

The aim is to maintain a steady course toward the winch.
During winching, the wing may drift to one side of the ideal tow path.
A small deviation can quickly grow if not corrected.
Therefore, alternate your attention between the wing and the position of the winch – the wing should fly directly toward the winch.

Correct deviations early and in a controlled way:

- Prioritize weight shift.
- Use the brake lines if weight shift is not sufficient.
- If you are already applying brake, you may need to release it on the side toward which you want to turn.

The wing is under more load during winching than during normal free flight.
Control inputs may therefore have a greater effect.
Be alert to overcorrection.

**If you veer off course**

The winch operator may reduce the pull if you veer off course.
This makes it easier to correct back toward the tow path.
Use the reduced pulling force to correct your course.
If the deviation becomes too large or you cannot correct it, the winch operator may terminate the pull for safety reasons.

**Movement in the harness**

Keep movements calm, especially during the first part of the tow.
Do not try to sit back in the harness until you have passed the first 100 critical metres of altitude.
Strong movements while getting into the harness can affect the wing and direction of flight.
If you cannot get into the harness easily, wait until you have sufficient altitude, a stable direction of flight, or have released from the winch line.

### 6.6 Release

When the winch operator terminates the pull and you are to release:

- Maintain control of the wing and direction of flight.
- Signal with your feet or use the agreed signal to indicate that you are about to release.
- When you feel the force decrease, release the line.
  Do this by letting go of one brake handle and pulling the release handle.
- Check that the line has actually released.
- Continue flying only when you are in control of the situation.

### 6.7 Line break at high altitude

If the line breaks at a good altitude, first prioritize control of the wing and direction of flight.

If the line or brake parachute is still trailing behind you, consider where it will land before releasing it.
If the terrain below is suitable, release as soon as you have control of the wing.
If you fly toward a safer area before releasing, be ready to release immediately if the line catches on something on the ground.

As far as possible, avoid dropping the line over roads, buildings, power lines, people or other activity.
Include this in your flight planning so you are prepared for the terrain and obstacles around you.

### 6.8 Line break at low altitude

A line break shortly after takeoff is a critical situation.
When the pulling force suddenly disappears, the wing may surge forward.

If the line breaks:

- Prioritize control of the wing.
- Be prepared for a surge and use the necessary brake to control the wing.
- Let the wing fly again once the surge is controlled.
- Make a gentle direction correction if needed and fly to a safe landing.

### 6.9 After landing

If you land back at the winch area:

- Stay clear of the active winch line and pilots preparing to launch.
- Move your wing and equipment out of the launch and landing area.
- Report to the launch assistant.

The launch assistant may give you feedback on the flight and put you on the list for the next tow.

### 6.10 Emergency procedures

In an abnormal situation, the pilot must always prioritize wing control and a safe direction of flight.

- If you are unsure before takeoff, abort the launch or release.
- If your direction of flight becomes dangerously misaligned, correct in a controlled way toward the winch.
- If the situation develops toward a lockout, release.
- If the line breaks, control the wing before deciding how to handle or drop the line.
- If the release does not work, use an alternative release method only if it is part of the approved system and your training.
- After an aborted tow at low altitude, prioritize flying speed, wing control and a safe landing.

Winching is teamwork.
Follow instructions from the launch assistant and winch operator, monitor the wing and winch throughout the towing phase, and help with the ground operation.
If you are unsure about equipment, procedures, signals or what to do in a situation, clarify it before connecting to the winch line.
