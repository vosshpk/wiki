# Board instructions

> **Status: consolidated draft — not confirmed as adopted.**
> This draft combines the contents of the two source documents “Instruks for styrets arbeid - v1 - v1 _1_ - v1.pdf” and “Instruks for styrets arbeid_1_ - v1.pdf”.
> Where the sources set different requirements, both alternatives are included below and identified for clarification.
> Check the instructions against the current bylaws, NIF rules and other rules that apply to the club before adoption.

## Purpose, scope and amendment

These instructions provide the board of Voss Hang- og Paragliderklubb with an orderly framework for effective and responsible handling of matters.
They bring together legal and bylaw requirements with internal working rules that the board must follow, and support club governance in accordance with NIF rules, the club's bylaws and other applicable rules.

The instructions take effect when adopted by the board.
The board may amend or repeal them by an ordinary majority decision, provided the decision does not conflict with rules that take precedence over these instructions.

## Board responsibilities

The board shall:

1. Ensure that the club is properly organised and that its administration has the routines, resources and competence to work towards established objectives and comply with applicable requirements.
2. Exercise overall control and oversight of the managing director and daily operations.
3. Keep informed about the club's financial position, obligations and development, and ensure that the accounts and management of funds are under proper control.
4. Handle matters that are unusual in nature or of major significance.
   This includes major investments or projects, substantial borrowing, major changes to operations and major agreements.
5. Handle the purchase, sale or mortgaging of real property and other decisions that the managing director has not been authorised to make.
6. Call the ordinary annual general meeting by 31 March, giving at least 31 days' notice and including the documents required by current law and bylaws.
7. Ensure that decisions made by the annual general meeting and higher-level organisational bodies are implemented.
8. Set objectives and a budget for the coming year by 30 November.
9. Evaluate the board's own work.
   This draft provides for an annual evaluation.
   One source says at least annually, while the other says at least every two years.
   The evaluation must cover the board's overall competence, way of working and meeting frequency.

## The managing director's role and authority

The board is the managing director's immediate superior.
The managing director reports to the board and keeps the chair informed between meetings when the matter warrants it.

The managing director leads ongoing operations and administration in accordance with legislation, board decisions, guidelines, instructions, the employment agreement and the managing director's instructions.
The managing director has the authority that naturally belongs to the role and may receive expanded authority from the board when needed.

Without specific delegated authority, the managing director must not decide matters that are unusual in nature or scope or of major significance.
This includes major investments, substantial borrowing, and the purchase, sale or mortgaging of real property.

## Reporting and cooperation with the board

The managing director shall:

- give the board an update on operations, financial position and performance at least monthly; the other source requires an update at least every other month;
- present periodic accounts and a liquidity budget;
- report on the market, products or services, organisation, major agreements, compliance with privacy rules (GDPR) and proposals for improvements;
- provide a more detailed account of specific matters when requested by the board;
- prepare matters together with the chair and ensure the decision basis is quality-assured;
- present a proposed meeting calendar with the chair at the first board meeting after the annual general meeting.
  The calendar must cover the next 12 months and show meeting dates, key matters and the proposed meeting format;
- implement board decisions and ensure employees and others involved receive the necessary information;
- notify the board if significant assumptions underlying earlier decisions change;
- immediately notify the chair of resignations by people in management or other key employees;
- notify the chair as soon as possible if the managing director cannot attend;
- notify the chair of their own absence for holidays or illness lasting more than three days;
- attend board meetings with the right to speak but not vote.
  The board may decide that the managing director must not attend a particular matter, such as a matter concerning the managing director;
- attend the annual general meeting and, as far as possible, be able to answer questions about finances, accounts and other matters for decision;
- call an extraordinary board meeting when an incident requires the board to meet.

The board shall evaluate the managing director annually.

## Meeting frequency and notice

The board shall meet at least every other month.
The chair ensures that matters belonging before the board, including unusual or important matters and major agreements, are considered.

The chair calls meetings.
The board handles matters in meetings.
The chair decides whether a meeting will be in person, online or a combination, and the meeting format must be stated in the notice.
The notice may be in Norwegian or English.
More than half of the board members may require the meeting to be held in a particular format.

The notice, agenda and all papers must be published in the club's digital board portal no later than seven days before the meeting.
Urgent circumstances may require a shorter notice period.
Matters requiring a decision must include a clear proposed resolution and an adequate basis for the decision.

Deputy members must receive the papers.
The sources differ on whether deputies are invited only when a board member is absent or whether all deputies are invited to every meeting.
If a board member is absent, the chair must in any case invite a deputy as soon as possible.

## Handling matters and decisions

The chair leads the meeting.
If the chair cannot attend, the board elects another person to chair that meeting.

The managing director, or a person appointed by the managing director, presents the matters.

The board may make decisions when more than half of its members are present and the matter is handled responsibly.
All board members must have had the opportunity to take part in handling a matter before a decision is made.
Check these requirements against the current bylaws and NIF rules.

Board members and the managing director must not take part in handling or deciding matters in which they have a conflict of interest.
This includes matters in which they or a close associate have a particular personal or financial interest, see section 2-8 of the NIF Act.

Decisions are made by a majority of those taking part in handling the matter.
In the event of a tie, the chair's vote decides if this is consistent with the current bylaws.

One source has an additional requirement for proposals that change an established state: those voting in favour must constitute more than one third of all board members.
This requirement must be checked against the current bylaws and NIF rules before it can be used.

## Evaluation at the end of the meeting

At the end of every meeting, the board must hold a review without the managing director or other administration staff.
The board must evaluate the meeting and what could be improved next time, discuss board dynamics and the organisation, and evaluate the managing director's performance.
After the meeting, the chair gives the managing director relevant feedback.

## Minutes

The minutes must include at least:

- the time, location or meeting format, and participants;
- the matters considered and decisions made by the board;
- a brief description of the discussion and decision basis when relevant;
- clear wording of each decision;
- whether the handling of matters complied with NIF rules and the club's bylaws;
- conflicts of interest and, when a decision is not unanimous, the names of those who voted for and against.

A board member or the managing director who disagrees with a decision may require their view to be recorded in the minutes.
The minutes must be available to board members no later than seven days after the meeting.

All board members who took part in handling the matters must sign the minutes.
The managing director and board members who did not attend must also sign to confirm that they have read the minutes.
The signed minutes must be stored in the club's digital board portal or another board archive approved by the club.

## Confidentiality

Board members must prevent sensitive information received through their role from being shared with unauthorised people.
Board discussions must be treated as confidential unless the board has decided otherwise.
Decisions may be communicated unless the board has imposed a restriction for the specific matter.

The board majority may impose confidentiality obligations on members for specific matters that require particular confidentiality.

## Matters to clarify before adoption

- This draft provides for an annual self-evaluation of the board, while one source says at least every two years.
  Confirm the frequency.
- The sources differ on whether deputies should be invited only when a member is absent or to every meeting.
  Set the practice; both sources say deputies must receive the papers.
- Confirm that notice of the annual general meeting must be given by 31 March and at least 31 days before the meeting, and confirm which documents current bylaws require.
- One source says the notice must include the annual accounts and any annual report and audit report, and that other matters required by law and the bylaws must be handled.
  It gives dividends, board remuneration and election of board members as examples.
  Clarify which of these apply to a sports club, and use annual-general-meeting and NIF terminology in the final text.
- Check the requirements for a quorum, the opportunity for all board members to participate, the chair's casting vote and the requirement for support from more than one third when changing an established state against current bylaws and NIF rules.
- Confirm whether more than half of the board members may require a particular meeting format.
- Confirm who must sign the minutes and whether board members who did not attend and the managing director must confirm that they have read them.
- Confirm the meeting frequency and regular reports the board will require from the managing director.
