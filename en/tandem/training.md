# training

This page gathers guidance on preparation, practice, tandem checkout and continued progression. It is based on [Voss HPK's tandem training guidance](https://vosshpk.no/nyhende/tandemutdanning/), published on 20 January 2026.

**Always check the latest NLF/HPS safety system and training programme.** Current requirements and procedures in those documents take precedence over this guidance. Contact the technical director before arranging practice or a checkout.

## Preparation

Tandem flying carries responsibility for the passenger, yourself and other people. Read the current safety system, training programme, tandem compendium, paragliding textbook and other relevant material from NLF/HPS and the club. Build a generous margin in your own skills and be confident that you can manage both the flying and the passenger before progressing.

The club article says two experienced pilots who hold PP4 or higher and have held their pilot ratings for at least two years may practise tandem together without a tandem certificate. NLF's guidance calls for preparatory training with PP4/PP5 pilots before checkout. Confirm who you can train with and what currently applies with the technical director or a tandem instructor. Do not start training on your own: incorrect technique can become a habit that takes time to correct.

Before the first practice flights, ask a tandem instructor to brief you on equipment, flight planning and how to conduct the flight. Arrange guidance before training starts, especially if you are unsure about common mistakes and risk traps.

## Practice tips

- Plan every phase of the flight carefully, including weather and local conditions.
- Use the first flights to get specific feedback from a tandem instructor or experienced tandem pilot.
- A tandem wing handles differently from a solo wing. Consider wing area, total weight and wing loading when assessing conditions.
- Double-check the equipment and passenger before launch.
- Set a clear stop point for the launch with enough margin to abort safely.
- When your instructor considers you ready, film take-offs and landings so you can review technique and identify improvements.

## Tandem checkout

Speak with the technical director before signing up. A positive initial assessment is not an approval: you must meet the requirements and demonstrate during checkout that you can fly safely and look after the passenger.

NLF's current guidance treats the five practical checkout flights as an assessment, not a course. The practical training must be completed beforehand. See [NLF's tandem course and checkout guidance](https://nlf.no/grener/hps/sikkerhet--utdanning/annet-laremateriell/om-tandem/) for the formal process, theory exam, Idrettskurs registration and technical director approval. Tandem instructor checkout and approval to fly for the club must follow the current renewal requirements.

Checkout assesses more than technical wing handling. You must demonstrate sound airmanship, strong risk assessment, reflection on your own decisions and the ability to manage difficult or stressful situations. Human factors and established risk-reduction methods are part of the preparation.

Voss HPK's guidance lists the following experience requirements for tandem pilots. Confirm each requirement against the current training programme before applying:

1. Hold a paragliding pilot rating for at least two years.
2. Hold the PP5 rating.
3. Be at least 18 years old.
4. Have at least 200 paragliding flights.
5. Have at least 100 paragliding flight hours.
6. Have at least five documented flights with another pilot as passenger, confirmed and approved by a tandem instructor.
7. Be approved by the technical director of a club where you have been a member for at least two years.

The theory covers meteorology, aerodynamics, the differences between tandem and solo flying, passenger briefings and communication. The theory exams require preparation; meteorology and aerodynamics are especially important. Read all required material before attending.

On checkout flights, the candidate flies while the instructor assists at launch and landing; the instructor does not fly as pilot or passenger. NLF's guidance expects error-free checkout flights and good wing control. Film the flights and review the footage afterwards. Bring suitable camera and selfie-stick equipment, and also film preparations such as rigging, fitting the passenger's harness, the briefing and launch practice. This makes it possible to review each phase in detail.

When possible, observe a club flight with an experienced pilot and passenger. Arrivals, paperwork, conversation, briefing and answering questions are a substantial part of tandem work; observing passenger care on the ground is useful preparation.

The tandem instructor responsible for checkout must be approved under current NLF/HPS rules. The club guidance assigns the safety group to appoint the responsible instructor, while the technical director organises and approves checkout for the club. Confirm who currently holds that mandate and follow NLF's formal process.

## After a successful checkout

After passing checkout and meeting the requirements, you can apply for a tandem certificate under the current procedure. The certificate permits you to fly with passengers who do not hold a pilot rating, within the limits of the rules and club procedures.

A tandem pilot's work includes more than flying. Arrivals, paperwork, talking and briefing the passenger, and answering questions are important parts of the job. Passengers may be inexperienced, nervous or react unexpectedly; include this in your risk assessment and training.

## Paid flights for the club

The club guidance calls for additional practice before paid flights. The local minimum is 15 logged tandem flights in total before a candidate can be considered for this work. This is in addition to the tandem certificate requirements and should be checked against current club decisions.

Flying with unfamiliar passengers requires experience, strong skills and motivation. Train over time and build experience before progressing. When you feel ready, contact the safety group for a new assessment. Starting paid flights also requires a contract, an introduction to the organisation, a briefing and clear expectations. The club should continue to support your progression.
