# Progression

Voss HPK recommends that pilots seek advice from instructors and experienced pilots throughout their progression. Ask questions and invite people to explain the reasoning behind their decisions; this helps build a supportive flying community. Check the [course overview](https://vosshpk.no/kurs/) for progression courses, which are added as they are scheduled.

## Practice

Know your privileges and which skills you may practise, then train regularly. Ground handling and kiting in safe surroundings are effective ways to improve wing control at every rating level. They are especially useful after a break from flying or when you have new equipment.

### Ground handling

Work calmly and in control, aiming for good wing control. Choose an area and conditions you can manage, with enough capacity to react. Identify obstacles, keep hold of the brakes and wear a helmet. Plan what you will do if the wind increases. Consider your distance from obstacles and whether you have enough space and time to stop if the wing pulls you. Being comfortable holding the wing overhead at a certain wind strength does not necessarily mean you can gather it quickly if the wind rises.

Wing control depends on balanced line pressure. Adjust speed and line pressure with the brakes, or with body position by walking or running forward and backward. A balanced wing stays still in the centre overhead: move beneath it and use the brakes to correct movement to either side, forward or backward. Maintain contact with the brakes while making inputs so you remain an active pilot.

At first, focus on one control at a time. Stand still and control the wing with the brakes, then try using only body position. Once you understand how the wing responds, add more exercises.

### Exercise bank

Voss HPK emphasizes building good habits and a sound foundation before experimenting. Use a suitable site and conditions you can manage. Discuss demanding exercises with an instructor.

- Bring a friend; it is more enjoyable and safer.
- Practise smooth inflations with gradual acceleration and gentle corrections. For an asymmetric inflation, brake the side that rises first.
- After a correction, wait for the wing to respond, then release the brakes back to neutral contact.
- Practise controlled forward and reverse inflations.
- Compare inflations with and without A-risers.
- Practise a calm transition from reverse to forward launch position.
- Try bringing the wing down with the brakes, C-lines and A-lines, and consider which works best for your wing.
- A fast inflation using A-risers makes the wing surge forward. The club suggests using this deliberately to practise pitch control, stopping the wing before a front collapse.
- In reverse inflations, practise bringing up the correct side first: the side with the upper riser should rise first. Stop if the wrong side rises first.
- Create wingtip vortices for each other to explore turbulence.
- Practise keeping the wing balanced in turbulence behind trees or other obstacles.
- Practise controlled wingtip touches from side to side.
- Kite the wing in both directions.
- Kite the wing straight backward using A-risers and body-position adjustments to line pressure.
- See who can keep the wing up longest without it touching the ground.
- See who can remain still longest before the wing touches the ground.
- Set up a slalom with objects or other pilots, maintaining distance and control.
- In moderate wind, observe downwind inflations and how much speed is needed to keep the wing overhead.
- The club also lists simulating a collapse by pulling different lines, using different trim settings, backflying and spins to both sides. These drills require appropriate skill, supervision and conditions; discuss them with an instructor before attempting them.

## SPG2 foot-launch course

To attend, a pilot must hold a valid SPG2 rating and have flown from at least five different sites. After passing the course, the pilot may foot-launch a speedglider. At a minimum, the course covers forward, reverse and aborted launches, a good flare and safe landing, and understanding the hazards of foot-launching a speedglider.

The requirement to pass a foot-launch course does not apply to holders of SPG2 student ratings issued before 29 November 2021. Confirm current requirements with an instructor and the latest training programme.

## From PP2 to PP3

A PP2 student has these privileges:

1. Ground handling on flat ground.
2. Glide flights from sites where the student has been checked out.
3. As the student approaches the next level, practice in simple lift is allowed only after an instructor has briefed and approved that specific flight.

Discuss new techniques and next-level requirements with an instructor before practising. Voss HPK highlights assessing conditions and making safe decisions, launching, approach patterns and landing. Its examples of basic wing exercises include clean turns up to 360 degrees without unwanted pendulum, one-sided collapses up to 30 percent and flying with speedbar.

Plan the approach early and begin it with enough height. The guide warns against launching in crosswind, tailwind, rough or strong wind, and against flying in unstable or turbulent conditions. Flying in lift requires prior briefing and approval from an instructor. If you cannot avoid entering lift without approval, fly straight through the lift band and away from terrain to calmer conditions, or land as soon as possible at the planned landing area.
