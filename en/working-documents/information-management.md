# Information Management

**Working document for the managing director and technical director.** This page sets out the privacy and archiving routine. Its aim is to give a simple overview of where information should be stored, who should have access, and what to remember along the way. Use it as practical support in daily operations, and update it when working methods or systems change.

The club uses its website, wiki, email and social media to communicate with members and others.
See the [internal communications plan](/en/working-documents/internal-communications-plan) and [external communications plan](/en/working-documents/external-communications-plan) for plans and responsibilities.
Official contact points are listed on the [about the club page](/en/the-club/about).

## Main rule

| Information | Storage location |
| --- | --- |
| Short, factual observation | Access-restricted overview in Google Drive |
| Detailed information about a person | A separate, access-restricted case in Orgbrain |

## Where information is stored

Store only information that is necessary, in the appropriate system, with access limited to people who need it for a specific task.

| Information | Storage location | Practical rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple incidents and safety observations | Access-restricted Google Drive folder | Keep entries short, specific and factual. Include a name only when individual follow-up requires it. |
| Concerns and detailed reports about people (for example, conduct, conflict or health) | A separate, access-restricted case in Orgbrain | Include only relevant information. Distinguish facts, information from others and personal assessments. Do not copy the full case to Drive or email. |
| Formal incident reports | The current NLF/HPS system | The internal overview does not replace formal reporting. |
| Tandem declarations | Restricted tandem archive on the club account | Check the form is signed before flying. Give the pilot only the information needed for the flight. Avoid detailed health information unless necessary. |
| Membership and course information | Federation system and necessary, access-restricted storage in Google Drive | Use only information needed for the task. |
| Board minutes and ordinary club documents | Orgbrain or the approved document archive | Grant access according to role and document type. |

## When an incident or concern is reported

Use these questions before storing or sharing information:

- Why is this being recorded?
- Is this a brief observation, or does it need further follow-up?
- Do we need a name to follow up?
- Who actually needs access?
- Is the information in the right place?

### Simple safety observations in Drive

The overview can be used throughout the season to identify patterns, follow up specific matters and produce anonymised statistics. Record, where possible:

- date, place and activity
- a short description of what was actually observed
- category or risk factor
- any action, owner and status
- a name only when individual follow-up is necessary

Avoid rumours, vague characterisations and unnecessary details. If a case needs a detailed account about a person, create a restricted Orgbrain case and keep only a short, necessary entry in the overview.

### Concerns and detailed reports in Orgbrain

- Create the case with restricted access from the start.
- Include only information relevant to handling the case.
- Clearly distinguish facts, information from others and personal assessments.
- Record who is following up, what has been done and when the case should be reviewed.
- Do not copy the full case to Drive, email or other folders.

### Tandem declarations

- Check that the declaration is signed before flying.
- Store the club's copy in the restricted tandem archive.
- The passenger may receive a copy of their own declaration.
- Give the pilot only the information needed to carry out and follow up the flight.
- Move attachments out of email once archived, and delete unnecessary email copies.

## Access and sharing

Access should follow the task, not the role alone.
Board members, the safety group and instructors do not automatically need access to every personal case.

- Use club accounts and two-factor authentication.
- Avoid shared passwords; use individual accounts and access groups where possible.
- Share only with people who need to handle or follow up the case.
- Use email for necessary transfer, not as a permanent archive.

## Regular cleanup routine

A short review after the season and an overall review by 31 January help keep the archives organised.

| What to review | What to do | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drive incident overview | Close follow-ups. Delete or anonymise names that are no longer needed, and prepare an anonymised annual overview. | After the season |
| Orgbrain cases | Review status, access and any continuing need for storage. Delete when there is no documented follow-up or legal need to retain the information. | When a case is closed and annually |
| Tandem archive | Review access and retention against its purpose, current NLF/HPS requirements, insurance and liability needs. | Annually |
| Accounts and shared folders | Remove access when people no longer need it for their work. Check two-factor authentication. | When roles change and annually |
| Email | Regularly delete archived attachments and copies that are no longer needed in the inbox. | Regularly |

## Responsibilities and incidents

### Who does what?

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Managing director | Oversees practical archiving, access, clean-up and incident follow-up. |
| Technical director | Follows up documentation and access in technical and safety work. |
| Board | Has overall responsibility and is involved when needed. This does not give every board member automatic access to personal cases. |

### If something goes wrong

Report a misdirected email, lost document, unauthorised access or other possible personal-data breach immediately to the managing director and board chair.

- Restrict access or stop the misdirected transfer as quickly as possible.
- Record what happened, what information was involved and who may be affected.
- Keep the necessary documentation about the incident.
- Assess whether the Norwegian Data Protection Authority and the affected people must be notified.

Keep what the club needs—no more, in no more places, and for no longer than necessary.
