# Internal communications plan

This plan is designed to strengthen internal communications in the club, promote cooperation between members and the board, and ensure that information flows well.
The aim is to build a strong organisation that works well internally and supports the club's overall vision.

## Objectives

1. **Effective flow of information:** Ensure that all members and volunteers have access to important information at the right time.
2. **Stronger sense of community:** Create a culture of openness, cooperation and sharing experience.
3. **Greater engagement:** Motivate members to take an active part in the club's activities and decision-making processes.

## Audiences

- The board
- Volunteers and elected officers
- Members (active and inactive)
- Instructors and course leads
- Working groups and committees

## Communication channels

We choose channels that are easy to use, accessible and effective.
The board should confirm which channels the club actually uses and what types of information belong in each channel.

### 1. Digital channels

- **Slack or Microsoft Teams:** For project collaboration, quick information sharing and discussion.
- **Members' Facebook group:** For informal communication, messages, and sharing photos and stories.
- **Email:** For official announcements, board minutes and documentation.
- **Google Drive or Dropbox:** For sharing important documents (annual reports, safety procedures and bylaws).
- **Member portal on the website:** For information about courses, resources and reservations of the club's equipment.

### 2. In-person channels

- **Member meetings:** Quarterly meetings for information, discussion and decisions.
- **Noticeboard at the clubhouse:** For local information, plans and notices.
- **Annual general meeting:** A forum for strategic decisions and reporting.

## Measures for effective internal communications

### 1. Clear roles and responsibilities

- **Action:** Clarify responsibilities for board members, committee chairs and volunteers.
- **Tool:** Share an organisation plan through the member portal.
- **Effect:** Reduce overlap and misunderstandings.

### 2. Regular updates

- **Actions:**
  - Weekly updates through Slack or Microsoft Teams about ongoing projects.
  - Monthly emails from the board with status updates and upcoming activities.
- **Effect:** Keep everyone informed about the club's progress.
The board should choose a realistic schedule and confirm which channels will be used for the updates.

### 3. A culture of feedback

- **Action:** Encourage two-way communication by:
  - Creating an anonymous feedback box, digital or physical.
  - Holding open question sessions with the board.
- **Effect:** Members feel heard and included.
Clarify how feedback is received and followed up.

### 4. Training and resources

- **Actions:**
  - Provide training in digital tools such as Slack and the member portal.
  - Prepare guides on safety, use of flying sites and equipment maintenance.
- **Effect:** Effective use of technology and greater competence among members.
Provide guidance for the digital tools the club actually uses and keep the guidance current.

### 5. Open decision-making

- **Actions:**
  - Publish board meeting minutes on the member portal.
  - Communicate strategic goals and plans at the annual general meeting.
- **Effect:** Build trust and engagement among members.
Respect privacy and matters that must be handled confidentially when sharing minutes and other information.

### 6. Social and inclusive activities

- **Actions:**
  - Arrange informal gatherings such as film nights, shared meals or trips.
  - Start a mentoring scheme for new members.
- **Effect:** Strengthen community and well-being.

## Responsibility for internal communications

1. **Communications lead:** Follows up to ensure internal communications work well.
2. **Board:** Ensures that important information is shared in time.
3. **Working groups:** Keep the board and members up to date on their projects.

The board should appoint the people responsible and confirm these roles.

## Review and adjustment

- **Measures:**
  - Responses to emails and activity in Slack or Microsoft Teams.
  - Attendance at member meetings and social events.
  - Feedback from members through surveys.
- **Schedule:** Review and adjust internal communications every six months.
The board should assign responsibility for the review and set a date.

## Conclusion

This internal communications plan is intended to strengthen Voss HPK as an organisation, ensure all members are well informed and build a strong and inclusive community.

## Procedure for communication during incidents

### 1. Immediate action during an incident

- **Notify the safety officer:** The person who observes or is involved in the incident notifies the club's safety officer immediately.
- **Incident reporting:** The safety officer must immediately start reporting the incident.
  This includes contacting relevant people and starting the incident report in accordance with the club's guidelines.
- **Insurance:** The safety officer must contact the insurance company at an early stage.

### 2. Internal notifications in the club

- **Safety group:** The safety officer notifies the safety group to ensure that the incident is handled and assessed in accordance with the club's safety system.
- **Technical director:** The technical director is informed if action is needed to prevent future incidents and to ensure relevant information reaches everyone responsible.
- **Club board:** The board must be informed about the incident so it is aware of any legal or organisational consequences.

### 3. External communication

- **Norwegian Air Sports Federation (NLF):** The technical director ensures that the report is sent to NLF if the incident requires it.
  This follows the applicable reporting requirements under the safety system.
- **Norwegian Civil Aviation Authority:** In cases with serious safety implications, NLF and the technical director assess whether the incident should also be reported to the Norwegian Civil Aviation Authority.

### 4. Information for club members

- **Information for members:** After an incident, hold an information meeting or distribute a report to inform all club members about the incident and the safety measures that will be implemented.
- **Safety meeting:** The safety group or technical director may arrange an extraordinary safety meeting if the incident reveals a need to improve existing safety procedures.

### 5. Follow-up and review

- **Debrief and learning:** After the incident has been handled, hold a debrief with the parties involved.
  Document lessons learned and suggestions for improvement.
- **Measures and improvements:** Implement any changes to the safety system or club procedures and communicate them to everyone affected.

This procedure ensures that the club handles incidents in a structured and safe way, with an emphasis on learning, safety and good communication.

## Contents of an early internal message to the board about a serious incident

1. **Confirm the incident:**
   - Briefly explain what happened, such as a flying accident, and where it happened, if relevant.
2. **Status:**
   - State that the extent of any injuries is unknown and that the emergency services have been notified.
3. **Actions taken so far:**
   - Describe measures taken, such as contacting the emergency services and looking after those involved.
4. **Further notifications:**
   - Emphasise that further notifications must follow the correct order and that speculation must be avoided.
5. **Privacy:**
   - Remind recipients that information must not be shared externally until more has been clarified.
6. **Updates:**
   - Confirm that more information will be shared as the situation develops.

### Example

> “A serious incident has occurred at [location].
> The emergency services have been notified, and the extent of any injuries is unknown.
> We are prioritising the care of those affected and will share more information as soon as we know more.
> Do not share this information further until the situation has been clarified.”
